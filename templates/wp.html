What is PSL-Scale?

1. Definition

The PSL-Scale is an informal rating system used in online communities to assess male facial attractiveness. Originating from forums like Lookism and Sluthate, it has become a core concept in the looksmaxing movement. While not scientific, it’s often used as a reference point for self-evaluation and aesthetic goals.


2. Levels of the Scale

Unlike the classic 1–10 attractiveness scale, the PSL-Scale uses more nuanced categories:
  • Sub 5: Severely asymmetrical or distorted features; often referred to as "troll tier"
  • Sub 3: Below-average appearance; weak bone structure, few attractive traits
  • LTN (Low Tier Normie): Slightly below average; not striking but socially acceptable
  • MTN (Mid Tier Normie): Average appearance with some appealing features
  • HTN (High Tier Normie): Above average; symmetrical face, good proportions
  • Chadlite: Attractive male with strong features, but not elite-level
  • Chad: Genetically gifted; strong jawline, symmetry, clear skin, high sexual appeal


3. Origins and Context

The scale emerged in 2024 on forums focused on dating, aesthetics, and self-improvement. It gained traction in the looksmaxing community, where users aim to enhance their appearance through grooming, fitness, and sometimes surgery.


4. Connection to Looksmaxing

Looksmaxing is the process of improving one’s appearance through intentional changes—such as skincare, hairstyle, posture, mewing, or cosmetic procedures. The PSL-Scale acts as a compass, helping users identify their starting point and set realistic goals.


5. A Word of Caution

The PSL-Scale is not scientific and should not be taken too seriously. Attractiveness is complex and influenced by many factors beyond facial structure. Over-focusing on appearance can be mentally exhausting, so it’s important to maintain a healthy self-image and perspective.
